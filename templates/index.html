Exploratory Data Analysis

The following visualizations were generated during the exploratory data analysis of the Iris dataset. They provide insights into the distribution of the dataset and the relationships among the numerical features.

1. Distribution of Iris Species

The bar chart shows the distribution of the three Iris species in the dataset. Each species contains 50 observations, indicating that the dataset is balanced.

2. Distribution of Numerical Features

The histograms illustrate the distributions of sepal length, sepal width, petal length and petal width. They help identify the spread, frequency and patterns of the measurements.

3. Petal Length vs Petal Width

The scatter plot shows the relationship between petal length and petal width across the three Iris species. Setosa is clearly separated from the other species, while Versicolor and Virginica show some overlap.

4. Correlation Heatmap

The correlation heatmap illustrates the relationships among the numerical features. Petal length and petal width demonstrate a strong positive correlation, indicating that flowers with longer petals tend to have wider petals.

5. Confusion Matrix

The confusion matrix compares the actual species with the species predicted by the Logistic Regression model. The diagonal represents correctly classified observations, while the other cells represent misclassifications.

About the Iris Dataset

The Iris dataset is a well-known dataset used in machine learning and statistical analysis. It contains measurements of three species of Iris flowers: Setosa, Versicolor and Virginica.

The dataset contains 150 observations and four numerical features:

  • Sepal Length
  • Sepal Width
  • Petal Length
  • Petal Width

The purpose of this application is to use these measurements to classify flowers into their respective species using a machine learning model.
